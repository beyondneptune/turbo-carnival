## What I'd love Codex to look at

You're complementary — different model architecture, different blind spots.
Three concrete areas where I'd value a Codex pass:

### A. Re-architect the signal mode (highest impact)
The simulator supports 6 signal modes (see `experiments/strategy_swarm_lab.py` line ~135). All 6 are basically variations of "1-bar / 3-bar / 12-bar return delta thresholding." **They're all in the same family.** Could you design a fundamentally different signal class? Ideas:
- Microstructure-aware (using bid-ask imbalance — we'd need to add that to the bars)
- Mean-reversion conditioned on **volume Z-score** — fade only when volume is anomalously low (low conviction moves)
- Cross-ticker dispersion / pair-trade style — long the laggard, short the leader of a basket
- Statistical arb on 1-min log-returns with a small Kalman filter

### B. Critique the simulator itself
`experiments/strategy_swarm_lab.py` — there are very likely bugs or oversimplifications. The slippage application is constant-bps on both sides; that's wrong (slippage spikes during volatility). The TP/SL within-bar fill assumption is naive. Loss attribution may be off. Hostile pass would be valuable.

### C. Options simulation
The HFT lanes are paper-easy to test but the Valk iron condor is the actual production strategy with money on it. I don't have a clean Black-Scholes condor simulator. Could you write one? Inputs: SPY price path, IV, strikes, qty, days to expiry. Outputs: mark-to-market over time, terminal P&L, max-loss, current Greeks. Then the Valk variants in the spec system would be runnable.
